# CrisisGrid — Antwerp Flood Scenario 01

**Status:** First draft / scenario requirements seed  
**Purpose:** Define a realistic, fictional flood incident in Antwerp that can drive CrisisGrid's first product slice, system architecture, user interfaces, simulations, and AI tools.  
**Scope:** This is not an operational emergency plan and must not be used for real-world response. It uses real geography and publicly documented flood context, but all incidents, readings, failures, operational decisions, capacities, and people below are fictional.

---

## 1. Scenario premise

CrisisGrid is deployed for a severe, compound flood threat affecting Antwerp and its surroundings.

The event combines:

- persistent rainfall across the Scheldt basin;
- high upstream river discharge;
- a North Sea storm surge coinciding with a high spring tide;
- intense local rainfall that overwhelms parts of the urban drainage network;
- disruption to transport, utilities, port-adjacent operations, healthcare access, and municipal services.

This is deliberately a **compound event**. It demonstrates why a real crisis platform must combine hydrology, weather, geography, infrastructure, field operations, public communications, policy controls, and multi-agency command rather than treating flooding as one isolated data feed.

Antwerp is a credible location for this scenario. The city lies on the Scheldt; storm surges can propagate into the estuary and high water levels are amplified near Antwerp by the estuary's narrowing geometry. Flemish flood protection combines strengthened dikes, flood-control areas and river-space measures under the Sigma Plan. Antwerp's Scheldt quays are also being adapted for higher storm-tide protection. [Sources](#sources-and-reality-check)

## 2. Design goals generated by the scenario

The scenario must force CrisisGrid to demonstrate:

1. **Early warning and uncertainty management** — forecasts, confidence, thresholds and changing predictions.
2. **A common operating picture** — one live operational map and timeline shared across organisations, while respecting access boundaries.
3. **Multi-agency coordination** — municipal, emergency, police, fire, medical, utility, transport and water-management actors.
4. **Field-to-command feedback** — mobile reports, photos, unit status, blocked routes and requests for support.
5. **Decision governance** — visible ownership, approval gates, escalation rules and auditable actions.
6. **Public warning** — drafted, approved and distributed messages with clear scope and channels.
7. **Graceful degradation** — the system continues operating when selected external feeds or integrations fail.
8. **Controlled AI assistance** — AI may summarize, correlate, propose and explain, but cannot take sensitive actions without explicit authority.
9. **Modularity** — new capabilities can be added as independently owned CrisisGrid modules with declared interfaces.
10. **A visually memorable live simulation** — water, weather, affected zones, units, infrastructure and decisions evolve over time on the real Antwerp map.

## 3. Geographic frame

### 3.1 Primary area of operations

The first prototype should use real map data for a bounded Antwerp operational area, not the entire metropolitan region.

**Recommended initial map extent:**

- the Scheldt river corridor through Antwerp;
- the historic centre and Scheldt quays;
- Linkeroever and the river crossings;
- selected low-lying or drainage-sensitive districts;
- one hospital area;
- one rail or road corridor;
- one industrial/port-adjacent risk location;
- designated shelters and command locations.

The exact operational zones, facilities and thresholds in the demo remain fictional. The objective is recognisability and realism, not a claim that the scenario reflects actual emergency vulnerabilities or response plans.

### 3.2 Map and visual-data approach

Use real base geography and openly available map layers where licence-compatible:

- **OpenStreetMap** for roads, buildings, neighbourhoods and points of interest;
- public elevation, hydrology and land-use datasets where suitable;
- optionally, a terrain/3D layer for the Scheldt, quays, bridges and skyline;
- synthetic overlays for flood extent, water depth, road closure, utility status, unit positions and emergency zones.

The operational map should be capable of both 2D command-centre use and a more cinematic 3D presentation mode.

## 4. Fictional operational setting

### 4.1 Incident name

**Operation High Water — Antwerp 01**

### 4.2 Time window

A 36-hour simulated incident, from early warning through stabilisation.

The first prototype should implement the first **12 hours** in depth. Later phases can be replayed from scripted event data.

### 4.3 Weather and water conditions

All values are fictional and intentionally not tied to real warning thresholds.

- A slow-moving Atlantic low-pressure system brings intense rainfall over the Scheldt basin.
- Forecast models indicate a potentially severe surge at the North Sea coast during a high-tide cycle.
- Upstream river discharge remains elevated.
- Local rainfall temporarily exceeds stormwater-drainage capacity in several districts.
- Water-level uncertainty remains material for the first several hours; the command team must act before the final outcome is known.

### 4.4 Impacted areas and assets

The initial scenario includes fictional versions of the following asset categories:

| Asset category | Scenario purpose |
|---|---|
| Scheldt quays and riverside public space | Visual flood risk, access restrictions and public safety |
| Road tunnel / bridge crossing | Transport disruption and routing decisions |
| Railway corridor or station access road | Cascading mobility impact |
| Regional hospital access route | Time-critical healthcare coordination |
| Care home | Vulnerable-person evacuation workflow |
| Primary school / sports hall | Potential reception centre or shelter |
| Electricity substation | Critical-infrastructure protection and potential outage |
| Water pumping station | Drainage and resilience dependency |
| Industrial site near river/port | Hazardous-material monitoring and escalation |
| Residential evacuation zone | Public warning, household status and welfare checks |

No real facility should be represented as currently unsafe, poorly protected or inadequately managed. Use neutral or fictional labels in the public demo where appropriate.

## 5. Organisations and roles

The scenario should model real categories of participants, but not attempt to reproduce Belgian command law or an actual chain of command.

| Actor | Core responsibility in CrisisGrid |
|---|---|
| Crisis coordination cell | Establishes objectives, approves major actions and receives situation reports |
| Municipal operations | Road closures, shelters, local infrastructure and public information |
| Fire and rescue operations | Rescue, pumping, hazard assessment and field coordination |
| Police / mobility operations | Perimeters, traffic control, evacuations and route safety |
| Medical coordination | Ambulance routing, patient transfer and hospital capacity |
| Water-management authority | Forecasts, water levels, flood-defence status and technical advice |
| Utility operators | Electricity, water, telecom and drainage status |
| Transport operator | Road, rail, tram or tunnel impacts and service changes |
| Field responders | Mobile observations, assignments, requests and completion reports |
| Public-information officer | Drafts and publishes approved warnings and updates |
| AI assistant | Summarizes, searches, correlates and proposes; cannot bypass policy gates |

## 6. Phase model

## Phase 0 — Baseline and preparedness

**Time:** before the event

CrisisGrid contains a normal-state model of the city:

- asset registry and critical-site classifications;
- responder units and capabilities;
- shelters and nominal capacity;
- approved public-warning templates;
- integration health status;
- roles, permissions and escalation policies;
- baseline maps and preconfigured operational layers.

This phase exists so the crisis does not begin from an empty screen.

## Phase 1 — Forecast and early warning

**Time:** 06:00–10:00

A forecast service emits a compound-flood risk alert.

### Key events

1. Rainfall and surge forecasts move from **monitor** to **elevated risk**.
2. Water-level and weather feeds disagree within a defined uncertainty band.
3. Flood-impact analysis identifies exposed infrastructure and vulnerable facilities.
4. A watch officer asks CrisisGrid for the likely operational impact.
5. The crisis coordination cell is notified and placed on standby.
6. The system proposes pre-positioning of selected resources and preparation of draft public messaging.

### Required system behaviour

- ingest simulated weather, water-level and forecast data;
- display confidence and time horizon, not only a single prediction;
- calculate and visualize affected zones;
- identify assets and populations intersecting risk zones;
- create a situation report draft;
- log who viewed, accepted, dismissed or escalated the forecast.

### Example natural-language interactions

- “Show critical facilities within the 12-hour high-risk area.”
- “What is the difference between the two forecast models?”
- “Prepare a briefing for the crisis coordinator.”
- “Which shelters are outside the predicted flood area and accessible by two routes?”

## Phase 2 — Incident activation

**Time:** 10:00–13:00

Water rises faster than the median forecast. Local surface flooding and pressure on key drainage assets are reported.

### Key events

7. A river gauge crosses a fictional pre-alert threshold.
8. A field crew reports water overtopping at a riverside access point.
9. A pumping station reports reduced capacity.
10. The command cell activates the incident at a defined level.
11. A preliminary evacuation zone is proposed.
12. Traffic operations receives a request to close a riverside road segment.
13. Public information prepares, but does not yet publish, a warning.

### Required system behaviour

- create a formal incident record and timeline;
- assign incident roles and operational objectives;
- generate tasks and dispatch units;
- keep proposals distinct from approved actions;
- show live status of field units and critical assets;
- issue integration-health warnings when a source becomes unreliable.

## Phase 3 — Evacuation and acute coordination

**Time:** 13:00–18:00

A projected flood zone expands. A care facility and hospital access route are at risk. The scenario requires a coordinated evacuation decision.

### Key events

14. Flood modelling expands the projected inundation area.
15. A care home reports that evacuation preparation is required.
16. One access route to a hospital becomes unreliable.
17. A traffic incident blocks an alternative road.
18. An industrial site reports a precautionary containment check.
19. The command cell approves a targeted evacuation.
20. A public warning is approved and sent through configured simulated channels.
21. Field responders report progress, blocked access and resident-assistance requests.
22. A mutual-aid request is generated for additional transport or pumping resources.

### Required system behaviour

- calculate a proposed evacuation zone and clearly mark it as a proposal until approved;
- support approval workflows with named authority and time-stamped rationale;
- coordinate transport, medical support, shelters and welfare checks;
- publish an auditable public-warning event;
- show route closures and safe access paths;
- allow operators to ask questions across structured and unstructured incident information;
- preserve a complete trace from forecast to decision to field outcome.

### Core proof slice

> A forecasted flood risk triggers a district evacuation. A commander approves a public warning. Field teams report progress and blocked roads. The command-centre map, timeline, architecture trace and audit record update in real time.

## Phase 4 — Peak and degraded operations

**Time:** 18:00–30:00

Water reaches peak levels. Multiple systems remain under pressure, and one external integration fails.

### Key events

23. Flood extent reaches a predefined peak area.
24. A utility site enters a precautionary shutdown state.
25. A legacy road-status feed becomes unavailable or stale.
26. CrisisGrid marks the feed degraded and switches to field-confirmed updates plus a fallback integration.
27. A public rumour or misinformation report requires a verified public update.
28. Hospital transfer coordination needs prioritisation under constrained routes.

### Required system behaviour

- distinguish verified data, estimated data and stale data;
- make degraded dependencies visible in both the operational view and architecture view;
- maintain safe operation with partial information;
- let the AI explain uncertainty and data provenance;
- require human approval for public notices and high-impact resource reallocations.

## Phase 5 — Stabilisation, recovery and review

**Time:** 30:00–36:00 and after

Water levels begin to decline. The focus shifts from life safety to restoration and learning.

### Key events

29. Evacuation orders are partially lifted.
30. Damage-assessment teams are dispatched.
31. Utility restoration work begins.
32. Roads are reopened in stages.
33. The command cell requests an after-action report.

### Required system behaviour

- manage recovery tasks and reopening decisions;
- preserve the full incident replay;
- generate an after-action report with decision history, communications, resource usage and integration failures;
- identify unresolved work and data gaps;
- support export of a synthetic audit package.

## 8. First application set

CrisisGrid should be implemented as multiple applications that consume and contribute to shared vmblu domains.

| Application | Primary users | First purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Command Centre | crisis coordinators, planners, commanders | Common operating picture, decisions, approvals and briefing |
| Field Response Mobile | responders and supervisors | Receive tasks, send observations, report status and request support |
| Simulation Console | developers/demo operator | Control clock, inject events, change feed quality and create replay runs |
| Public Information Console | communications officer | Draft, approve, publish and audit public messages |
| Integration Sandbox | developers/integration owners | Emulate water, weather, utility, mobility and sensor feeds |
| Architecture Explorer | developers, stakeholders, agents | Inspect modules, interfaces, message traces, policy gates and failures |

A simplified public or executive viewer may be added later. It should never expose sensitive operational information.

## 9. Initial vmblu domain model

The first architecture should be organised as independently extensible domains.

| Domain | Responsibility | Key events/commands |
|---|---|---|
| Flood Intelligence | forecasts, gauges, weather, flood extent and uncertainty | `forecastUpdated`, `gaugeThresholdCrossed`, `floodExtentProjected` |
| Risk Assessment | asset exposure, impact scoring, vulnerable sites and scenarios | `assessImpact`, `riskIdentified`, `zoneRecommended` |
| Incident Command | incident lifecycle, objectives, decision records and escalation | `activateIncident`, `approveDecision`, `incidentStatusChanged` |
| Operations Dispatch | units, tasks, routes, status and mutual aid | `createTask`, `assignUnit`, `fieldStatusUpdated` |
| Mobility & Access | road conditions, closures, route safety and transport requests | `closeRoute`, `routeStatusUpdated`, `alternateRouteProposed` |
| Care Coordination | hospitals, care sites, patient/evacuation transport and shelters | `requestEvacuationSupport`, `capacityUpdated`, `transferPlanned` |
| Public Warning | templates, drafts, approval and simulated delivery | `draftWarning`, `approveWarning`, `publishWarning` |
| Infrastructure Status | utilities, pumps, substations, communications and recovery | `assetStatusUpdated`, `serviceDegraded`, `restoreService` |
| Integration Gateway | external feeds, adapters, health, staleness and fallbacks | `integrationHealthChanged`, `sourceDataReceived`, `fallbackActivated` |
| Identity, Policy & Audit | roles, permissions, data labels, approvals and evidence | `authorizeAction`, `recordAudit`, `accessDenied` |
| Common Operating Picture | map layers, timeline, operational widgets and subscriptions | `overlayUpdated`, `timelineEventAdded` |
| AI Assistance | grounded query, summarisation, extraction and recommendation | `askOperationalQuestion`, `generateBriefing`, `proposeAction` |

The design principle is that a future module — drone coordination, hospital capacity, telecommunications, volunteer management, flood-defence engineering or cross-border support — can be added by declaring its interfaces, policy needs, UI contribution and health behaviour, without modifying every existing module.

## 10. Policy and security constraints for the demo

The public demonstration should make security visible rather than treating it as an implementation detail.

- Every action is associated with an actor, authority, reason and timestamp.
- High-impact actions require a policy gate and named approval.
- Data has at least a simple sensitivity classification: public, operational, restricted.
- AI tools receive narrow, declared capabilities rather than general database access.
- AI output is marked as generated, linked to its sources and cannot publish warnings or dispatch units by itself.
- Sensitive information must not appear in public or executive views.
- All locations, unit identifiers, people, vulnerabilities and operational values used in the demo are synthetic unless openly published and intentionally included.

## 11. AI-assisted workflows for the first slice

AI is a controlled supporting service, not the central product feature.

### Must-have capabilities

1. **Situation summariser**
   - Produces a concise summary of what changed since a selected time.
   - Links each claim to the underlying event, map layer or report.

2. **Impact question answering**
   - Answers questions over the live incident model.
   - Example: “Which critical sites now have only one viable access route?”

3. **Field-report extraction**
   - Converts a free-text or voice-transcribed field report into a proposed structured observation.
   - A human confirms before it becomes operationally authoritative.

4. **Public-warning drafting**
   - Generates a draft using approved templates and current incident facts.
   - Publication remains an explicit human-approved workflow.

### Not in the first slice

- autonomous evacuation orders;
- autonomous dispatch of emergency units;
- unbounded access to external systems;
- predictive claims presented as certainty;
- face recognition, person tracking or sensitive personal-data analysis.

## 12. Visual direction

The visual presentation should have two connected modes.

### Operational mode

A clear command-centre interface:

- 2D/3D map of Antwerp;
- water and flood overlays;
- unit locations and task status;
- incident timeline;
- alert and approval queue;
- critical-infrastructure health;
- public-warning status;
- AI briefing panel with citations to live system data.

### Architecture mode

A vmblu view that is entered from real operational events:

- click a field report or public warning;
- reveal the message path across domains;
- show policy gates, data provenance, integrations and fallback route;
- show which module owns each responsibility;
- replay the path as the scenario evolves.

The essential experience is that the audience sees both **the city under pressure** and **the executable architecture coordinating the response**.

## 13. Acceptance criteria for the first demonstrable vertical slice

The first milestone is complete when a user can run the first 12-hour scenario and demonstrate all of the following:

1. Start, pause, accelerate and replay the flood simulation.
2. See a forecast, uncertainty indicator and projected risk zone on a recognisable Antwerp map.
3. Activate an incident and assign operational roles.
4. Identify a fictional care facility and hospital access route as exposed.
5. Generate, review and approve a targeted evacuation decision.
6. Draft, approve and simulate publication of a public warning.
7. Receive mobile field reports and update unit/task status in real time.
8. Simulate a failed or stale road-status integration and show safe fallback behaviour.
9. Ask a natural-language question grounded in incident data and inspect the sources used.
10. Open the vmblu architecture view and trace one end-to-end flow:

    `forecast → risk assessment → incident activation → evacuation approval → public warning → field response → audit trail`

## 14. Questions to resolve before detailed specification

1. Which real Antwerp areas should be visually recognisable in the first map extent, while avoiding any implication that named sites are actual weak points?
2. Should the first major threat be primarily storm surge, river discharge, local pluvial flooding, or the compound combination described here?
3. Which public data layers can be reused directly, and which should be represented synthetically for predictable demos?
4. What level of 3D is required for the first milestone: stylised terrain and city massing, or detailed assets around the quays and river crossings?
5. Which mobile workflow should be first: field observation, task completion, evacuation support, or infrastructure inspection?
6. Which real-world standards and public documentation should shape terminology and interoperable data contracts?
7. How much Belgian institutional specificity is desirable before the project becomes too narrow for an international audience?

## Sources and reality check

The scenario is fictional. The following sources are used only to establish that Antwerp and the Scheldt estuary provide a credible real-world context for a flood-management simulation:

- [Sigma Plan — About the Sigma Plan](https://www.sigmaplan.be/en/about-sigma-plan): Flemish flood-protection programme; storm surges, dikes and flood-control areas.
- [Sigma Plan — Scheldt Quays](https://www.sigmaplan.be/en/projects/scheldt-quays): Antwerp's exposure to heavy storm surges and adaptation of quays.
- [European Climate Adaptation Platform — Scheldt estuary flood protection](https://climate-adapt.eea.europa.eu/en/metadata/case-studies/an-integrated-plan-incorporating-flood-protection-the-sigma-plan-scheldt-estuary-belgium): integrated Sigma Plan context.
- [European Climate Adaptation Platform — Hedwige and Prosper polders](https://climate-adapt.eea.europa.eu/en/metadata/case-studies/a-transboundary-depoldered-area-for-flood-protection-and-nature-hedwige-and-prosper-polders): explains why storm surges can be significant at Antwerp.
- [Deltares — self-closing flood barrier testing](https://www.deltares.nl/en/news/worlds-longest-self-closing-flood-barrier-tested-deltaflume): current engineering work for storm-tide protection at the renewed Scheldt quays.
- [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/): candidate base-map source, subject to its licence and attribution requirements.
